Privacy policy

This page explains what personal information ST Publishing collects, why, who else handles it, and how you can use your privacy rights. It covers our website, editionsst.com, and the sign-up forms we run on Facebook and Instagram. Une version française, rédigée pour le Québec, est disponible : Politique de confidentialité.

Last updated — September 20, 2026

01 — Who we are

ST Publishing is the English-language imprint of Éditions ST, an independent publisher based in Québec, Canada. It is run by Sylvain Tremblay as a sole proprietorship, and it decides why and how the personal information described here is used. This policy also applies at stpublishing.ca, which redirects to editionsst.com/en.

For any question or request about your personal information, email us at info@editionsst.com. Mailing address: CP 60065, CSP Faubourg, Boisbriand, Québec, Canada J7H 0G7.

Our privacy officer — the person responsible for protecting personal information under Québec's Law 25, and accountable for it under Canada's PIPEDA — is Sylvain Tremblay, reachable at the same email address.

02 — What we collect

We collect only what you choose to give us, and it isn't much:

  • your first name, your last name (optional) and your email address, when you ask for the free guide or join the newsletter. You can do that two ways: the sign-up form on editionsst.com, or a Facebook or Instagram (Meta) lead form shown in our ads. With a Meta form, Meta may prefill the fields from your profile; you can edit them before you submit;
  • the language of the sign-up page, when we know it, so we write to you in yours;
  • your message and address, if you email us — used only to reply.

The website itself has no user accounts and takes no payments: purchases happen at Books.by or Amazon, and their own policies apply once you leave our site. Browsing data (cookies, the pixel, your IP address) is covered in section 05.

03 — How we use it

We use your information to:

  • send you the free guide you asked for;
  • write to you now and then, through the newsletter, about new releases from the house — nothing else, and only when there is something to announce;
  • reply to your messages;
  • measure how well our advertising works, if you accept the Meta pixel (section 05).

Legal basis: your consent. Through the website form, signing up means ticking the consent box and then confirming through a link we email you: without that click, you receive nothing. Through a Meta lead form, submitting it is your request to receive the guide and the newsletter. Either way, every message we send carries an unsubscribe link that works in one click.

We do not sell your information and we do not share it with third parties for their commercial purposes. It is used for nothing beyond what is described here.

04 — Who handles your information

We rely on service providers to run all this. These are the ones that touch your information, and why:

  • EmailOctopus Ltd (United Kingdom) — hosts our subscriber list and sends the guide and the newsletter.
  • Meta Platforms, Inc. (United States) — hosts the Facebook and Instagram lead forms: your answers pass through Meta before they reach us, and Meta also handles them under its own policy. Meta also supplies the advertising pixel (section 05).
  • Pabbly Connect (Magnet Brains Software Pvt. Ltd., India) — an automation service that moves sign-ups received through Meta lead forms (first name, last name if given, email) into EmailOctopus. Pabbly says it stores its data with Amazon Web Services (United States) and Hetzner (Finland).
  • Cloudflare, Inc. — cookie-free audience measurement, no persistent identifier.
  • FreeIPAPI — infers country and region from the IP address, on two pages only; a German company, servers in Europe.
  • Google LLC — font delivery, and the reCAPTCHA service on the website's sign-up form.
  • HeyGen — hosts the video embedded on the pages for L'écart, c'est vous. and The Gap Is You.
  • GitHub, Inc. (Microsoft) — website hosting.

These providers are based, or store their data, outside Québec — notably in the United States, the United Kingdom, India, Finland and Germany. Your information may therefore be processed in those countries, under their own rules. Nothing is sold, and we share no data for any purpose beyond what is described here.

05 — Cookies, the Meta pixel and browsing data

The Meta (Facebook) pixel

The pixel lets us measure the site's audience and show targeted ads on Meta's platforms. As a rule, it loads only if you click "Accept" in the consent banner: until you agree, no data is shared with Meta. Legal basis: your consent.

One exception, on two pages only: the English free-guide page (editionsst.com/en/free-guide/) and the sign-up confirmation page (/merci/). If you are visiting from the United States, or from a Canadian province other than Québec, the pixel loads as soon as you arrive on those pages, without a prior click. A notice then appears and you can opt out at any time (the notice's "Opt out" button, or the "Cookies" link at the bottom of the page): opting out stops the pixel and is remembered. Everywhere else — Québec, the European Union and other countries — and whenever your location can't be established reliably (a VPN, a service error, a timeout), the pixel loads only after you agree. Legal basis: your consent (Québec, the European Union and other countries); notice with a right to opt out (the United States and Canada outside Québec).

Your consent choice

Whether you accept or decline, that choice is stored only in your browser's local storage — never on a server — so you aren't asked again on every visit. That technical information is strictly necessary for the banner to work and is not itself subject to consent.

How to limit cookies and tracking

  • The "Cookies" button at the bottom of every page reopens the consent banner, where you can change your choice at any time.
  • In your browser (Chrome, Safari, Firefox, Edge) you can block third-party cookies, or clear cookies and site data. Clearing local storage for editionsst.com also resets your consent choice.
  • A content blocker, or private browsing, also limits what the pixel can collect.
  • In your Facebook or Instagram account, the ad preferences and "off-Meta activity" settings let you restrict how that data is used to target you.

Location from your IP address (FreeIPAPI)

On those two pages, your browser queries the FreeIPAPI service to infer your country and region from your IP address, which sends it that address. We keep only the country, the region and a flag for a virtual private network from the response, in your browser's session storage until you close the tab — not the IP address, not the coordinates. This inference is used only to apply the consent rule that matches your region. Legal basis: legitimate interest in complying with that rule. It is approximate: it does not prove where you live.

FreeIPAPI is operated by a German company (Düsseldorf), and the servers of its free plan are in Europe: your IP address is therefore sent outside Québec, to Europe. Its policy provides for request logs kept for monitoring and debugging, without stating what they contain or how long they are kept, and for possible sharing of information with service providers and partners. Éditions ST does not control those logs.

Cookie-free audience measurement (Cloudflare Web Analytics)

Cloudflare Web Analytics measures site traffic without a cookie or any identifier that follows you between visits or across sites. Because it cannot identify you, it loads on every page without going through the consent banner — unlike the Meta pixel. Legal basis: legitimate interest in audience measurement.

Fonts (Google Fonts)

The Spectral and IBM Plex Mono fonts used on the site are served from Google's servers, which shares your IP address with Google when the page loads. Legal basis: legitimate interest in displaying the site.

Embedded video (HeyGen)

On the pages for L'écart, c'est vous. and its English edition, The Gap Is You., a presentation video is embedded from HeyGen's servers. Loading it shares technical information with that service, under its own policy. Legal basis: legitimate interest in those pages' functioning.

The website sign-up form (EmailOctopus and reCAPTCHA)

The form appears on the catalogue, book and free-guide pages. Displaying it also loads Google reCAPTCHA, which screens out automated sign-ups and receives technical information about your browser for that purpose, under Google's own policy.

Hosting logs

The site is hosted on GitHub Pages (GitHub, Inc. / Microsoft), which keeps standard technical logs, including IP addresses, as part of its own service. Éditions ST has no access to those logs beyond what GitHub makes available.

The only audience-measurement tools we use are the Meta pixel and Cloudflare Web Analytics, and the only ways we collect your information are the sign-up forms (website and Meta) and email — all described above.

06 — How long we keep it
  • Newsletter sign-up — kept for as long as you stay subscribed: until you unsubscribe, or until you ask us to delete it.
  • Meta lead forms — your answers are kept by Meta under its own rules, outside Éditions ST's direct control.
  • Pabbly Connect transfer history — under its policy, task history is available only for the last 15 days.
  • Meta pixel — under Meta's own retention periods, outside Éditions ST's direct control.
  • Consent choice — kept in your browser until you clear it or change your choice.
  • Country and region inferred from your IP address — kept in your browser's session storage until you close the tab.
  • FreeIPAPI logs — under its own retention periods, which it does not specify, outside Éditions ST's direct control.
  • Emails you send us — kept as long as needed to deal with your request.
07 — Your privacy rights

In Canada. We handle personal information in line with Québec's Law 25 (which modernized the Act respecting the protection of personal information in the private sector) and, for the rest of Canada, the federal Personal Information Protection and Electronic Documents Act (PIPEDA). Wherever you live in Canada, you can at any time:

  • ask for access to the personal information we hold about you;
  • ask us to correct it if it is inaccurate or incomplete;
  • withdraw your consent — through the one-click unsubscribe link in every message or, for the Meta pixel, the "Cookies" button;
  • ask us to delete it;
  • ask for a portable copy, where the law provides for it.

To use these rights, email info@editionsst.com. Our privacy officer, Sylvain Tremblay, will answer within 30 days of receiving your request. If you believe your rights are not being respected, you can complain to the Office of the Privacy Commissioner of Canada or, in Québec, to the Commission d'accès à l'information.

In the United States. Depending on your state, privacy laws may give you rights to access, correct and delete your data, and to opt out of targeted advertising. Sharing data with Meta through the pixel can count as targeted advertising under some of those laws — that is why you can opt out at any time, as described in section 05. We do not sell your personal information for money. Write to us at the address above and we will handle your request.

Elsewhere. If you are in the European Union or the United Kingdom and the GDPR applies to you, you can also complain to the data protection authority in your country.

08 — Changes to this policy

We may update this page if the processing described here changes — for example when we add a new tool or a new service provider. The date at the top shows the latest revision.
